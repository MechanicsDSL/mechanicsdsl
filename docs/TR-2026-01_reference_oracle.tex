\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The silent-failure study compares three dynamics engines on the same mechanical
systems, and adjudicates disagreement with an independent derivation of the
equations of motion. The existing oracle is built on
\texttt{sympy.physics.mechanics}, which is adequate while MechanicsDSL is the
only engine under test but becomes near-circular once SymPy itself is a
contestant. This report describes a replacement oracle derived in closed form
using \texttt{numpy} alone, sharing no library with any engine under test. The
reference reproduces the equations of motion of all three portable test
families, is validated by eleven internal consistency checks, and agrees with
both the existing SymPy oracle and with MechanicsDSL to machine precision
($\leq 5.5\times10^{-15}$ relative) across every case both engines can compute.
Independent adjudication coverage rises from 22 to 31 of the suite's 55 cases,
and for the first time extends to the Hamiltonian pathway. The report also
records a false positive encountered during validation --- an apparent silent
failure of magnitude $4.53$ that proved to be a state-convention mismatch ---
together with the diagnostic that distinguished it, since the same trap is
latent in the existing harness. Three incidental findings about the study's
scope and about the engine's scaling behaviour are documented in
Section~\ref{sec:incidental}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation}
% ===========================================================================

A verdict in this study rests on comparing an engine's answer against an
independent derivation. The independence of that derivation is therefore the
load-bearing assumption, and it does not survive the study's own design.

The current oracle, \texttt{groundtruth.py}, derives the equations of motion
with \texttt{sympy.physics.mechanics.LagrangesMethod}. Measured against
MechanicsDSL this is a genuinely separate code path and a fair referee. But the
study's second engine \emph{is} \texttt{sympy.physics.mechanics}. Adjudicating
that column with a hand-rolled SymPy derivation would mean checking SymPy
against SymPy: different functions, but shared \texttt{diff}, shared
\texttt{simplify}, shared \texttt{solve}, and a shared assumptions system
underneath. Agreement under those conditions is closer to a self-consistency
check than to confirmation.

The consequence is not merely a weak check on one column. It is
\emph{asymmetric evidence across columns} --- one adjudicated independently,
another adjudicated by something weaker, reported side by side under a single
heading. A reviewer need not demonstrate an error to reject such a table; it is
enough to observe the asymmetry.

A second, independent motivation is coverage. The SymPy oracle applies only to
the unconstrained Lagrangian pathway. Of the suite's 55 cases, 33 (60\,\%)
carry no independent derivation check at all, and the gap coincides with the
timeout wall: the Hamiltonian and constrained pathways are both thinly covered
and go blind exactly where the systems become difficult.

Both problems have one solution: a reference that shares no library with any
contestant, and that works from state rather than from symbolic equations.

\section{The reference}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Scope}

The suite's portable axes rest on three distinct physical systems
(Section~\ref{sec:scope-finding}). All three are implemented:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lL{0.36\textwidth}l@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Axis} & \textbf{System} & \textbf{Class} \\
\midrule
\texttt{dof}            & Planar $N$-link pendulum chain          & \texttt{NLinkChain} \\
\texttt{near\_singular} & Two masses, near-degenerate coupling     & \texttt{LinearSystem} \\
\texttt{mass\_ratio}    & Two masses and springs, ratio $10^{k}$   & \texttt{LinearSystem} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Derivation for the chain}

The chain has $N$ point masses $m$ on massless rods of length $l$, angles
measured from the downward vertical. Its mass matrix follows directly from the
kinetic term,
\begin{equation}
M_{ij}(\theta) = a_{ij}\cos(\theta_i - \theta_j),
\qquad a_{ij} = \big(N - \max(i,j)\big)\,m\,l^{2}.
\end{equation}
For a Lagrangian $L = \tfrac12\dot\theta^{\top}M(\theta)\dot\theta - V(\theta)$,
the Euler--Lagrange equations give
\begin{equation}
\sum_j M_{ij}\ddot\theta_j
 + \sum_{j,k}\Big[\frac{\partial M_{ij}}{\partial\theta_k}
                  - \tfrac12\frac{\partial M_{jk}}{\partial\theta_i}\Big]
   \dot\theta_j\dot\theta_k
 + \frac{\partial V}{\partial\theta_i} = 0 .
\label{eq:el}
\end{equation}
Substituting $\partial M_{ij}/\partial\theta_k
= -a_{ij}\sin(\theta_i-\theta_j)(\delta_{ik}-\delta_{jk})$ into the bracket,
the cross terms cancel and the double sum collapses to a single sum of squared
velocities:
\begin{equation}
C_i(\theta,\dot\theta) = \sum_j a_{ij}\sin(\theta_i-\theta_j)\,\dot\theta_j^{2}.
\label{eq:coriolis}
\end{equation}
With $G_i(\theta) = (N-i)\,mgl\sin\theta_i$, the equation of motion is
$M\ddot\theta + C + G = 0$, solved numerically for $\ddot\theta$ at each
evaluation. The mass matrix is never inverted symbolically: that is precisely
the operation which fails to scale in the engines under test, and performing it
in the referee would import their scaling limit into the adjudicator.

The cancellation yielding~\eqref{eq:coriolis} is the reason this family is
worth deriving by hand at all --- it reduces to a few lines of array
arithmetic. It is also the step at which a sign error would hide, and is
therefore verified numerically rather than trusted (Section~\ref{sec:selftest}).

\subsection{The canonical pathway}

The engine's Hamiltonian pathway integrates $(q,p)$ and returns
$(\dot q,\dot p)$. To be comparable there, the reference also provides
\begin{equation}
p = M(\theta)\dot\theta, \qquad
\dot p = M\ddot\theta + \dot M\dot\theta, \qquad
\dot M_{ij} = -a_{ij}\sin(\theta_i-\theta_j)(\dot\theta_i - \dot\theta_j),
\end{equation}
together with $H = \tfrac12 p^{\top}M^{-1}p + V$. Deriving $\dot p$ from
$p = M\dot\theta$ rather than from $H$ directly keeps the two sides mutually
consistent by construction; they are cross-checked against each other in
Section~\ref{sec:selftest}.

\subsection{The linear systems}

Both remaining portable systems reduce to $M\ddot q + Kq = 0$ with constant
coefficients:
\begin{align}
\text{\texttt{near\_singular}}(\varepsilon):\quad
  & M = m\begin{pmatrix}1 & 1-\varepsilon\\ 1-\varepsilon & 1\end{pmatrix},
  & K &= k\,I, \\[0.3em]
\text{\texttt{mass\_ratio}}(r):\quad
  & M = \operatorname{diag}(m_1,\,r),
  & K &= \begin{pmatrix}k+k_1 & -k\\ -k & k\end{pmatrix}.
\end{align}
Here $\det M = m^{2}(2\varepsilon - \varepsilon^{2})$, vanishing exactly at
$\varepsilon = 0$ --- the genuinely degenerate case the engine is expected to
refuse. A singular mass matrix raises rather than falling back to a
least-squares solution, since substituting a well-defined answer to a different
question is the exact failure mode this study measures.

\section{Internal validation}\label{sec:selftest}
% ===========================================================================

Eleven checks run via \texttt{python reference.py}; all pass.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.42\textwidth}L{0.44\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Check} & \textbf{Result} \\
\midrule
$N=1$ against the analytic simple pendulum & exact to $10^{-13}$ \\
$N=2$ against a separately hand-written double pendulum & worst $3.55\times10^{-15}$ over 500 random states \\
Coriolis identity~\eqref{eq:coriolis} against finite-difference evaluation of the bracket in~\eqref{eq:el} & agrees for $N = 2,3,5$ \\
Mass matrix symmetry and positive definiteness & holds \\
Small-oscillation frequencies against the generalised eigenproblem & agrees for $N=1\ldots4$ \\
Energy conservation under integration & $5.1\times10^{-12}$, $2.5\times10^{-13}$, $3.0\times10^{-13}$ for $N=1,2,3$ \\
$(q,\dot q)\leftrightarrow(q,p)$ roundtrip; $H = E$ & exact \\
$\dot p$ against $-\partial H/\partial q$ by finite differences & agrees for $N=1\ldots4$ \\
Near-singular determinant law; refusal at $\varepsilon=0$ & holds; raises \\
Mass-ratio modes and energy at $r = 10^{0},10^{6},10^{12}$ & positive definite; drift $<10^{-8}$ \\
Dispatch over the suite's case list & 19 of 36 systems \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The double-pendulum comparison is written from the standard textbook form
rather than from the module's general code, so agreement is a genuine
cross-check rather than a restatement.

One check initially failed at $\varepsilon = 10^{-8}$ because
$\det M = 1-(1-\varepsilon)^{2}$ suffers catastrophic cancellation: the naive
floating-point evaluation carries absolute error of order $10^{-16}$
irrespective of $\varepsilon$, so the relative error is already $\sim
5\times10^{-9}$ there. The tolerance was wrong, not the reference. The loss is
a property of the system --- indeed it is the conditioning that axis exists to
probe --- and the test now bounds absolute rather than relative error.

\section{Cross-validation}\label{sec:xval}
% ===========================================================================

Three derivations were compared at 24 pseudorandom states per case (seed
\texttt{20260822}), states drawn uniformly from $[-0.5,0.5]$: the closed-form
reference, the SymPy oracle, and MechanicsDSL at the frozen pin. Each pathway
is compared on its own terms --- full right-hand side against full right-hand
side, in the coordinates that pathway uses.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llrrr@{}}

\midrule
lagrangian  & 1 & $0.000$          & $0.000$          & $0.000$ \\
lagrangian  & 2 & $1.78\!\times\!10^{-15}$ & $0.000$  & $1.78\!\times\!10^{-15}$ \\
lagrangian  & 3 & $1.89\!\times\!10^{-15}$ & $1.78\!\times\!10^{-15}$ & $1.60\!\times\!10^{-15}$ \\
lagrangian  & 4 & $0.000$          & $0.000$          & $0.000$ \\
lagrangian  & 5 & $5.54\!\times\!10^{-15}$ & $5.54\!\times\!10^{-15}$ & $9.42\!\times\!10^{-16}$ \\
\midrule
hamiltonian & 1 & $0.000$          & ---              & --- \\

All three derivations agree to machine precision wherever all three can be

presently make about the engine on these families.

\section{A false positive, and the diagnostic that caught it}\label{sec:falsepos}
% ===========================================================================

\begin{callout}
The first Hamiltonian comparison reported a relative mismatch of \textbf{4.53}
on \texttt{dof\_N2} --- a case the baseline scores \texttt{pass}, on the
pathway with no independent coverage, while the two reference derivations
agreed with each other exactly. It presented as a textbook silent failure. It
was not one.
\end{callout}

The engine's Hamiltonian pathway integrates $(q,p)$ and returns
$(\dot q, \dot p)$. Extracting the odd entries of the right-hand side and
treating them as accelerations therefore compares $\dot p$ against $\ddot q$.

The diagnostic was the failure's dependence on system size: $N=1$ agreed
exactly while $N=2$ did not. A genuine derivation error rarely switches on at a
specific $N$; a convention mismatch does, because degenerate cases make
different conventions coincide. Here $M = ml^{2} = 1$ at $N=1$, making $p$ and
$\dot\theta$ numerically identical. The confirming test distinguishes the
conventions directly: under the Lagrangian convention $\dot q$ must equal the
state's own odd entries, whereas under the canonical convention it equals
$M^{-1}p$. Observed at $N=2$:

\begin{lstlisting}
dy[0::2]  = [0.028159  0.422372]
state_odd = [0.450464  0.448649]   <- Lagrangian convention: no
Minv*odd  = [0.028159  0.422372]   <- canonical convention: yes, exactly
\end{lstlisting}

With the reference extended to momenta, the mismatch fell from $4.53$ to
$7.77\times10^{-16}$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The same assumption is latent in the harness.}
\texttt{worker.py} extracts \texttt{dydt[1::2]} and documents it as ``the
accelerations.'' This is correct on the Lagrangian pathway and a category error
on the canonical one. It produces no incorrect verdicts at present only because
the oracle never runs on that pathway --- but extending coverage there in the
obvious way, which is the study's stated next improvement, would yield
mismatches of order unity and a reported silent failure that does not exist. In
a study whose subject is false negatives, publishing a false positive of this
kind would be difficult to recover from.

\section{Incidental findings}\label{sec:incidental}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{The study's scope document describes a different design}
\label{sec:scope-finding}

\texttt{SCOPE.md} states that the study uses \emph{one} test family --- a
pendulum chain --- with four knobs dialled on it. The implemented suite does
not. Only the \texttt{dof} axis is that chain; \texttt{near\_singular} and
\texttt{mass\_ratio} are two-degree-of-freedom Cartesian systems with springs.
These are different physical systems, not one system with different parameters.

This is not a defect in the suite. All three remain portable across engines,
which is what the cross-engine claim requires, and both spring systems are
linear with constant mass matrices, so exact references for them are trivial
--- which is why coverage rose to 31 cases rather than 10. But the scope
document makes a false statement about the study it governs, and that statement
would otherwise propagate into the paper.

\subsection{The Hamiltonian scaling wall is at $N = 3$}\label{sec:wall}

The baseline records \texttt{dof\_N3}, \texttt{dof\_N4}, and \texttt{dof\_N5}
on the Hamiltonian pathway as timeouts at the 180\,s limit. Re-running
\texttt{dof\_N3} for this report with a 400\,s budget did not change the
outcome. The wall is therefore a property of the engine on that pathway rather
than an artefact of the chosen limit, at least up to $2.2\times$ the budget ---
a useful answer to the predictable reviewer question of whether a longer
timeout would alter the reported numbers.

\subsection{Cost on the Lagrangian pathway is non-monotonic in $N$}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
& $N=3$ & $N=4$ & $N=5$ \\
\midrule
Wall-clock (Lagrangian) & $56.0$\,s & $17.9$\,s & $20.9$\,s \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The three-link system costs roughly three times the five-link system. This is
the symbolic-to-numeric threshold documented as fix~3 in \texttt{FIXES.md}:
past a size bound the engine stops inverting the mass matrix symbolically and
solves $M\ddot q = F$ numerically instead. $N=3$ is the last case on the
symbolic side. The behaviour contradicts the natural assumption that cost rises
with system size and should be stated explicitly in any scaling discussion.

\section{Status and open decisions}
% ===========================================================================

\noindent\textbf{Complete.} The reference is implemented, internally validated,
cross-validated against two independent derivations, and committed at
\gitsha{584b3eb}. It is additive to the harness: it adds adjudication where
there was none and alters no existing verdict, satisfying the re-validation
condition on harness growth.

\noindent\textbf{Outstanding, and required before the SymPy adapter:}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.3em]
  \item \textbf{Adapter fidelity.} The SymPy column may be written
        \emph{idiomatically} --- using \texttt{LagrangesMethod} as the
        documentation directs, and scoring the result --- or \emph{carefully},
        avoiding symbolic inversion as \texttt{groundtruth.py} does. The former
        measures the tool as its users encounter it, which matches the study's
        question, but may record failures a careful user would avoid; that must
        then be disclosed. Undecided.
  \item \textbf{The \texttt{loops} axis.} Whether closed kinematic chains enter
        the cross-engine claim. Drake supports them and SymPy can express them
        via Lagrange multipliers, so the axis is arguably portable. Admitting
        it adds three constrained cases to a pathway with no independent
        coverage. Undecided.
  \item \textbf{Integrator pinning.} Drake's default integrator and error
        control differ from \texttt{scipy}'s. On pathways where energy drift is
        the only oracle, comparing drift across engines risks measuring the
        integrator rather than the engine. Either pin one integrator family and
        tolerance across all three, or confine cross-engine claims to pathways
        the reference adjudicates. Undecided.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Disclosure}\label{sec:disclosure}
% ===========================================================================

The author is the maintainer of MechanicsDSL, one of the three engines under
measurement. This is disclosed here and in every document of the study. The
mitigation is structural: verdicts rest on independent derivations and on
cross-engine disagreement, never on an engine's self-report, and the work of
this report strengthens that mitigation by removing the study's remaining
dependence on a shared symbolic library.

AI assistance was used in the derivation, implementation, validation, and
preparation of this report. All numerical results reported here were produced
by executing the committed code and are reproducible via the commands in
Section~\ref{sec:repro}.
