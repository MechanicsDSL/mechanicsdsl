To the Editors, Journal of Systems and Software

Dear Editors,

I am submitting "Limits of Differential Testing: Three Rigid-Body Dynamics
Engines Under Library-Independent Adjudication" for consideration as a research
paper.

Differential testing rests on the premise that independent implementations fail
independently. Knight and Leveson showed that premise is unreliable when the
correlation comes from shared human misconception. This paper reports a failure
correlated for a different reason, and one that no amount of implementation
diversity can address: it originates in floating-point arithmetic rather than in
anyone's code, and is therefore exhibited by every implementation, including a
reference written from first principles specifically to be independent.

The contribution is methodological. I exhibit a concrete failure invisible to
the method, identify the property that makes it invisible, and give a reusable
design -- adjudication by a referee sharing no derivation code with any
implementation under test -- for telling shared failures from independent ones.
The argument is an existence proof rather than a frequency estimate, and the
manuscript says so explicitly in its threats to validity. Section 1.4 states the
three research questions the study answers.

Four points a reader may wish to know in advance.

First, the study includes a correction to its own earlier result. An apparent
capability separation between the engines proved on examination to measure how
each engine had been driven rather than what it could do; under equal budgets
the separation disappears. The retraction is reported in the text rather than
quietly applied, and Section 1.7 withdraws an argument the manuscript previously
made on the strength of it.

Second, I maintain one of the three engines evaluated. This is declared
formally, and mitigated structurally rather than by declaration alone: no engine
adjudicates itself or any other, the case matrix was frozen before measurement
under a rule forbidding removal of cases once scored, and the reported results
include every measurement on which my engine performs worst.

Third, one asymmetry in the comparison -- a family on which one engine cannot
share the pinned integrator -- was characterised in a public discussion with
that library's maintainers, who confirmed the account given in the threats to
validity. The manuscript cites the thread so that a reader can check the
limitation against its source rather than take my description of it.

Fourth, all data and code needed to reproduce every figure and table are
archived at doi:10.5281/zenodo.22315172 under the MIT licence, together with the
raw output records from which each reported figure was computed.

On length: the manuscript is 19 pages in the journal double-column format,
marginally above the 18-page guidance. The additional page is the reproduction
map in Appendix A, which maps each reported result to the script that produces
it and the record it is stored in. I judged that a study whose contribution is
methodological is better served by making every claim independently checkable
than by removing that table, but I am happy to move it to supplementary material
if the editors prefer.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has not
been published previously. I have used generative AI in both the research and
the writing, declared in the two places Elsevier's policy requires.

Thank you for your consideration.
