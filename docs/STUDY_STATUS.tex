{\LARGE\bfseries Silent-Failure Study}\\[0.3em]
{\large Status as of 25 August 2026 \quad$\cdot$\quad \textbf{Week 1 complete}}\\[0.2em]
{\color{rulegrey}\hrule height 1pt}

\vspace{0.7em}

{\large\bfseries Where you are:} all three engines are built, validated, and
compared. Two claims, and they came out differently:\\[0.3em]
\textbf{(A) Engines can report success while returning wrong physics, unwarned
--- \textcolor{okgreen}{SUPPORTED}.} At the inverted equilibrium all three
engines \emph{and} the referee return $\sim$20\,rad of motion for a system that
does not move, conserving energy the whole way, so every check passes it.\\[0.2em]
\textbf{(B) Engines \emph{differ} in whether they do this ---
\textcolor{holdred}{NOT supported}.} Every engine behaves identically
everywhere measured, including in (A).

\vspace{0.4em}

\section{Also found}

\begin{tabular}{@{}cL{0.90\textwidth}@{}}
\textcolor{holdred}{\textbf{!}} & At the inverted equilibrium ($\theta=\pi$) all three engines \emph{and} the referee report success while producing $\sim$20\,rad of motion where the exact answer is none. Cause is $\sin\pi = 1.22\times10^{-16}$ in float64, amplified by an unstable equilibrium. Not an engine defect --- but nobody warns you. A shared blind spot. \\
\textcolor{holdred}{\textbf{!}} & Two apparent silent failures were \emph{my} coordinate-convention errors, not engine defects: the canonical $(q,p)$ state, and Drake's relative joint angles. Both vanished at $N{=}1$ and grew with $N$ --- the signature of a convention mismatch. Check that pattern before reporting any disagreement. \\
\textcolor{holdred}{\textbf{!}} & Three study documents described the study inaccurately and were corrected: \texttt{SCOPE.md} claims one test family (there are three); the portable-axes list named a non-existent axis; the instrument-freeze rule forbade Week 1. \\
\end{tabular}
