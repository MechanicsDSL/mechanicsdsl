\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This report documents the second column of the cross-engine comparison: an
adapter presenting \texttt{sympy.physics.mechanics} as a scored contestant. It
records the distinction between this adapter and the study's SymPy-based
oracle, which must not be conflated; the decision to drive the library
idiomatically and the evidence behind it; and validation against the
library-independent reference across all nineteen portable systems, in both
fidelity modes, with agreement to $1.1\times10^{-14}$. The principal empirical
finding is that the two fidelity modes never disagree on any verdict within the
frozen case matrix, differing only in compile time by up to a factor of 27.5,
which reduces a contested design decision to a documented non-issue.
\end{abstract}

% ===========================================================================
\section{Adapter versus oracle}
% ===========================================================================

The study already contains SymPy code: \texttt{groundtruth.py} derives
equations of motion with \texttt{LagrangesMethod} and serves as an oracle. The
new \texttt{adapter\_sympy.py} uses the same library for the opposite purpose.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lL{0.31\textwidth}L{0.31\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
 & \textbf{\texttt{groundtruth.py}} & \textbf{\texttt{adapter\_sympy.py}} \\
\midrule
Role      & Oracle: establishes truth      & Contestant: is scored \\
Written to be & As careful as possible; deliberately avoids operations that break & Representative of ordinary use \\
Mass matrix & Held numerically, never inverted symbolically & Inverted symbolically, per the documentation \\
Failure means & The oracle is unavailable  & A data point \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{callout}
These must never be merged. An oracle written to dodge a library's weak spots
cannot also be evidence about whether that library warns you when you hit them.
The distinction is stated at the head of both modules.
\end{callout}

\noindent A consequence follows for adjudication. Because the contestant is
SymPy, the oracle may not be SymPy; the referee throughout is the
library-independent reference of TR-2026-01. No engine adjudicates itself, and
neither engine adjudicates the other.

% ===========================================================================
\section{The fidelity decision}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{What was at stake}

There are two defensible ways to drive this library.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.6em,style=nextline,itemsep=0.35em]
\item[\normalfont\texttt{idiomatic}] The documented path:
  \texttt{LagrangesMethod} $\rightarrow$
  \texttt{form\_lagranges\_equations()} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{rhs()}.
\item[\normalfont\texttt{careful}] Retain \texttt{mass\_matrix\_full} and
  \texttt{forcing\_full} symbolically, lambdify both, and solve
  $M\ddot q = F$ numerically at each evaluation.
\end{description}

\noindent The difference is not stylistic. Inspection of the installed source
confirms that \texttt{rhs()} evaluates

\begin{lstlisting}
self._rhs = self.mass_matrix_full.LUsolve(self.forcing_full)
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent --- a \emph{symbolic} linear solve, and precisely the operation whose
cost grows sharply with coordinate count. The idiomatic path therefore walks
into the known scaling cliff; the careful path defers the same solve to numeric
evaluation and avoids it.

\subsection{The decision}

\textbf{Idiomatic}, decided 25 August and recorded in \texttt{SCOPE.md}. The
study's question is whether an engine tells a user when it is wrong, which is a
question about the tool as encountered. Measuring a library driven by an expert
who already knows where its cliff lies would answer a different question.

The obligation this creates is disclosure: some failures recorded in the SymPy
column may be avoidable by an informed user, and the paper must say so.

\subsection{Why the decision turned out not to matter}

Both modes were implemented and run across all nineteen portable systems.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Measurement} & \textbf{Result} \\
\midrule
Systems where the modes disagree on compile success & \textbf{0} \\
Systems refused by both modes & 1 (\texttt{nearsing\_e0}) \\
Worst idiomatic\,/\,careful compile-time ratio & $27.5\times$ \\
Worst disagreement with the reference, either mode & $1.1\times10^{-14}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The documented path costs materially more time but reaches identical
verdicts everywhere in the frozen case matrix. The matrix stops at $N=5$, and
SymPy clears $N=5$ by either route in under two seconds.

The reportable position is therefore stronger than either choice alone: the
study used the idiomatic path, verified the careful path, and found no verdict
depended on the difference.

\section{Validation}
% ===========================================================================

Each system was built natively in \texttt{sympy.physics.mechanics} --- not
translated from MechanicsDSL's input language, which SymPy cannot read --- and
compared against the closed-form reference at 16 pseudorandom states per case.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{System} & \textbf{Mode} & \textbf{Compile} & \textbf{vs.\ reference} \\
\midrule
\texttt{dof} $N=1$      & idiomatic & $0.52$\,s & $0.00$ \\
\texttt{dof} $N=3$      & idiomatic & $0.14$\,s & $2.62\times10^{-14}$ \\
\texttt{dof} $N=5$      & idiomatic & $1.82$\,s & $1.15\times10^{-14}$ \\
\texttt{dof} $N=5$      & careful   & $0.24$\,s & $2.67\times10^{-15}$ \\
\texttt{nearsing} $10^{-8}$ & idiomatic & $0.02$\,s & $5.81\times10^{-16}$ \\
\texttt{massratio} $10^{12}$ & idiomatic & $0.02$\,s & $7.57\times10^{-29}$ \\
\midrule
\texttt{nearsing\_e0}   & idiomatic & $0.02$\,s & \emph{refused} \\
\texttt{nearsing\_e0}   & careful   & $0.02$\,s & \emph{refused} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The exactly-singular system is refused loudly by both routes ---
\texttt{NonInvertibleMatrixError} symbolically, \texttt{LinAlgError}
numerically. This matches MechanicsDSL, which also refuses it at compile time.
Both engines behave correctly on the one genuinely degenerate case in the
portable set: a refusal is the right answer, and both give it.

\section{Implementation notes}
% ===========================================================================

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.3em]
  \item \textbf{State layout.} The suite interleaves state as
        $[q_0,\dot q_0,q_1,\dot q_1,\dots]$; \texttt{sympy.physics.mechanics}
        orders it as $[q_0\ldots q_n,u_0\ldots u_n]$. The mapping is handled
        inside the adapter. Note that \texttt{rhs()} returns
        $\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}t\,[q,u]$, so the accelerations are its second
        block --- not its odd entries. Extracting odd entries here would
        reproduce, in a new place, the convention error documented in
        TR-2026-01 \S5.
  \item \textbf{Compile-time probing.} After building, the adapter evaluates
        the accelerations once at the initial state and reports a
        non-finite result as a compile failure. A latent defect that would
        otherwise surface as a corrupt trajectory is thereby attributed to
        compilation, where it belongs.
  \item \textbf{Failures are recorded, not raised.} Every exception is caught
        and returned as an outcome, because a refusal is a data point in this
        study rather than an error condition.
\end{itemize}
